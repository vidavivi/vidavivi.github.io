Ongoing · XJTLU · 2025–present

DOMI: embodied support for stress regulation

A soft breathing companion paired with VR. I’m investigating how an individualized EEG-derived recovery state can guide the level of visual, auditory, and haptic support.

My work: EEG pilot, state-to-support mapping, prototype integration, and study design.

Research details

A 13-participant pilot with 32-channel EEG informed the choice of frontal alpha as a candidate recovery-related signal. The prototype coordinates breathing cues in VR with the companion’s abdominal motion.

The planned study compares EEG-contingent support with matched non-contingent support. The adaptive system’s effects on recovery and experience remain to be evaluated.

Closed-loop EEG–VR system

SURF 2025 · XJTLU

MindGarden: a VR forest that responds to brainwaves

A real-time system connecting wearable EEG to an AI-generated relaxation environment, where flower size and color respond to physiological feedback.

My work: system development, EEG processing, and a counterbalanced user study.

Research details

I built a streaming pipeline connecting Emotiv Insight, MATLAB, and Unity through Lab Streaming Layer, using the beta/alpha ratio to drive visual feedback in Meta Quest 3.

In a 10-participant study, closed-loop feedback produced the lowest reported stress and higher hedonic ratings; paced breathing showed the clearest EEG relaxation trend.

ICVR 2025 · First author

Visuo-haptic VR for stress relief

Combining AI-generated felt-style nature with soft tactile feedback to examine how synchronized visual and haptic experiences can support relaxation.

My work: project conception, Unity–Arduino prototyping, three studies, and physiological evaluation.

Research details

Three studies examined acceptance of AI-generated content (N = 176), visual–haptic experience (N = 37), and dynamic visuo-haptic synchrony (N = 15).

The final system synchronized VR camera motion with a pneumatic felt device. Descriptive results favored synchronized feedback across relaxation and restoration measures.

Manuscript under review · ISCAS · First author

How do we measure experience in teleoperation?

A scoping review of subjective evaluation practices, examining which constructs researchers measure and how they select, adapt, and report instruments.

My work: review leadership, coding framework, analysis, and evidence synthesis.

Research details

The review covers 187 publications reporting 196 studies and 661 study–construct–measure records.

I developed a coding framework spanning 13 subjective construct categories. The review distinguishes standardized, adapted, and custom measures, and examines how constructs vary with tasks, interfaces, and experimental factors.

The synthesis informs context-sensitive measure selection and more transparent reporting.

MIND 2026 · Accepted for oral presentation

Reliable EEG decoding across feedback contexts

Investigating how feedback accuracy affects subject-independent decoding of error-related potentials in a controlled motor-imagery BCI paradigm.

My work: experimental design, EEG processing, and decoding benchmarks.

Research details

The benchmark includes 12 participants and 3,174 valid EEG trials.

The experiment compared 60.0%, 73.3%, and 90.0% feedback-accuracy contexts. I benchmarked shrinkage LDA, xDAWN–Riemannian logistic regression, and EEGNet using leave-one-subject-out validation.

Mean balanced accuracy ranged from 69.9% to 71.3% across the decoding approaches.
